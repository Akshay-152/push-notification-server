Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026

What push notifications are

When you enable notifications, your browser creates a push subscription: a randomly generated delivery address hosted by your browser vendor's push service (for example Mozilla's or Apple's), plus encryption keys. We store that subscription so we can send you messages. The messages are encrypted end-to-end: only your browser can read them.

What we store

  • The push subscription endpoint and its encryption keys.
  • An anonymous random identifier (a cookie) that lets this browser manage its own subscription — unsubscribe, check status.
  • Your browser's user-agent string and subscription timestamps.
  • Delivery outcome records (accepted / failed) for our own statistics. These never contain the message content of anything you read — only whether the push service accepted a message for your subscription.

What we do not store

  • No name, email address, or account — subscriptions are anonymous.
  • No precise location or device fingerprinting.
  • We never sell or share subscription data with third parties.

Purpose

We use subscriptions solely to deliver the notifications you agreed to receive and to keep our subscriber statistics accurate (for example, removing subscriptions that no longer exist).

Retention & deletion

Subscriptions are removed automatically once they expire or are reported gone by the push service. You can delete yours instantly with the Unsubscribe button on the home page, which removes it from our database and unsubscribes your browser.

Cookies

We use two strictly necessary cookies: your browser session for administrators, and the anonymous subscription identifier described above. No tracking or advertising cookies are used.
